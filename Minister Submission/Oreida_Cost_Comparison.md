## 1. The price proposed

| Item | Amount | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **One-off licence and installation charge** | **US\$2,800,000** | Design, build, deployment and officer training — the platform as delivered |
| **Monthly service charge** | **US\$72,000 per month** | Hosting, running, power, connectivity, support, maintenance and updates |
| **Term** | **10 years, reviewed at year 5** | With the platform and all data reverting to the Government at the end |

**One price, two tools — and this must be said plainly.** The licence and the monthly service charge cover **both** tools: the **policy-simulation assistant**, which tests a draft policy against the groups it reaches, and the **research assistant**, which turns ZEPARI's published evidence into answers, briefs and a policy brief. **Either can be used entirely on its own; they complement each other. Nothing is charged per tool, and nothing is charged per officer.**

**The payment steps for the one-off (all inside the build period at the start — not years, and not the ten-year term):**

| Step | Amount | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Licence fee on signature | US\$1,300,000 | Payment for the right to use an asset that already exists and works today |
| Mobilisation advance on signature | US\$500,000 | Against a bank guarantee |
| On acceptance of server-side document text extraction | US\$300,000 | Written acceptance |
| On acceptance of server-side identity verification | US\$300,000 | Written acceptance |
| On acceptance of deployment and pilot live | US\$400,000 | Written acceptance |
| **Total** | **US\$2,800,000** | |

**These figures are the price proposed by Oreida Pvt Ltd. They are not approved and not agreed.**

## 2. The one-paragraph answer to "is this reasonable?"

**Yes, and the strongest evidence is Zimbabwe's own.** The Ministry of Finance is, on the World Bank's own published record, paying **approximately US\$3 million a year** simply to maintain one financial system — and that system was appraised at **US\$20 million** to improve it. Against that, **US\$2,800,000 to buy a working, sovereign policy-assessment platform outright, and US\$864,000 a year all-in to have Oreida Pvt Ltd run, host, power, maintain and support it, is small.** The one-off is **14%** of the Ministry's own appraised figure for one system, and the yearly cost is **29%** of what the Ministry already pays in maintenance alone — for a platform covering **16 institutions**. Every figure below is named and published; nothing here is an invented estimate of ours.

## 4. How this compares with comparable national platforms (over ten years)

Redone like-for-like over ten years (build + ten years of published annual running cost):

| Country / platform | Build | 10 years of running | **10-year total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mauritius | US\$1.45M | US\$3.00M | **US\$4.45M** |
| Tunisia | US\$4.80M | US\$1.55M | **US\$6.35M** |
| Uganda *(contract terminated 2021)* | US\$1.55M | US\$8.73M | **US\$10.28M** |
| **Nzwisiso AI — ours** | **US\$2.80M** | **US\$8.64M** | **US\$11.44M** |
| Rwanda *(210 bodies, 2,000 users)* | US\$7.80M | US\$8.00M | **US\$15.80M** |

**Ours sits third of five over ten years, and is materially cheaper than Rwanda for a platform serving 16 institutions against Rwanda's 210.** A fair reading of these comparators is that the price is within the published African band for national digital-government platforms, not above it.

*A genuine limit, stated plainly: these comparators are for **government administrative platforms** (e-procurement, financial management, digital government). They are a fair yardstick for a policy platform of this kind. They are not a claim of exact equivalence, and no figure here is presented as an exact match.*

## 5. What the Government gets for the price

| Benefit | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A standardised policy-making procedure | ZEPARI's own documented finding that policies fail because *"there is no standardised procedure to policy making"* |
| A delivered evidence base, not a promise | 16 institutions, 510 cited reference indicators, 150 stakeholder groups, 228 published documents (125 departmental + ZEPARI's 103) |
| Sovereignty | A sovereign engine and a local model, so policy text need not be sent to an external service in the costed end state |
| Reversion | The platform **and all data** revert to the Government at the end of the term; the source code is held in escrow |
