## 1. Why this, and why now

The Government has already decided that Zimbabwe will digitalise public administration — **Vision 2030**; **NDS2 (2026–2030)**; **Digitalize Zimbabwe** (launched by the Honourable Minister at Domboshava in April 2024, with a stated horizon of 2030); the **Presidential Internet Scheme**, approved by Cabinet in April 2025 for all **2,400** administrative wards; and the **National AI Strategy** launched in June 2026, whose named priority sectors are healthcare, agriculture, education and financial inclusion. What does not yet exist is a working, sovereign example the Ministry itself can put to work. This platform is exactly that, one step short.

## 2. The problem it addresses

Policies are implemented without a structured way of examining how the groups they affect are likely to respond, so the cost of a provision that is not understood — resistance, delay, rework, or a measure that cannot be delivered as written — is discovered **after** implementation. Departments hold the expertise, but no standard tool lays out in one place who is affected, which provisions carry the greatest risk, and what to adjust.

## 3. Two tools — independent, complementary, one price

**Nzwisiso AI™ Policy Simulation Assistant** tests a draft policy against the **40** stakeholder groups a department's mandate covers, and produces a structured assessment a person can read, challenge and act upon. **ZEPARI Research Assistant™** turns ZEPARI's own published library into answers, briefs and a policy brief. **Either can be used entirely on its own** — and they complement each other: the Simulation Assistant tests the policy, the Research Assistant supplies the evidence behind it. The boundary is deliberate: the Research Assistant models nothing, and **no figure from the research side is ever fed into the simulation engine**, which is what keeps *"the same policy always produces the same result"* true. **One price covers both tools — no charge per tool, per department, per officer or per use.**

## 4. What the Government gets, and what ZEPARI gets

**The Government** gets a working platform today, in an ordinary browser, with nothing installed: **16 institutions**; **510 reference indicators** (275 published figures, each naming its publisher, publication and period; 235 labelled `Modelled`); **150 modelled stakeholder groups** (20 standing on a published national share, 130 labelled `Modelled`); **48 prepared policy drafts**; **49 cited reference documents**; and a complete officer journey ending in PDF, Word or print export and an editable drafted policy. Results are byte-identical for the same department and the same policy text, so a finding is defensible in a meeting. **ZEPARI** gets its research finally used in the policy process, attributed every time, reaching offices it cannot reach itself, with its independence preserved and visible — and its own 2012 recommendation, the "knowledge utilisation approach", delivered fourteen years later.

## 5. The figures, and where they come from

The one-off licence is **US\$2,800,000** and the monthly service charge is **US\$72,000**, for a **ten-year term reviewed at the fifth year**, at the end of which the platform and all data revert to the Government. The one-off is paid in five part-payments as work is accepted — **US\$1,300,000** licence fee on signature and **US\$500,000** mobilisation advance against a bank guarantee, then **US\$300,000 + US\$300,000 + US\$400,000** on the three build acceptances. **Both figures sit inside published ranges:** Zimbabwe's own financial-management project was appraised at **US\$20 million** and signed at **US\$10 million**, with about **US\$3 million a year** to maintain that system alone *(World Bank, Zimbabwe: Public Financial Management Enhancement Project, ICR00005623, 4 February 2022)*, and comparable national platforms elsewhere in Africa cost **US\$1.45 million to US\$7.8 million** to build with **US\$155,000 to US\$873,000** a year to run *(World Bank, Electronic Government Procurement — Implementation Types: Options for Africa, 2022)*.

**Named-source statement.** Stakeholder shares are the **Zimbabwe National Statistics Agency (ZIMSTAT)** 2022 census figures, each naming the figure it stands for, or labelled `Modelled` where no official count exists; reference rates name their publisher and period; every cited instrument is a real Zimbabwean Act; and every indicator either names its publisher, publication and period or is shown as `Modelled`. **What a simulation produces is a projection, not a measurement, and is always labelled simulated.**
