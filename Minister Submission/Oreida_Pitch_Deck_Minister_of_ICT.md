# The problem

- Policies are implemented without a structured way of examining who they affect and how those groups are likely to respond.
- The cost of a provision that is not understood — resistance, delay, rework — is discovered **after** implementation, not before it.
- Departments hold deep policy expertise, but they have no standard tool that lays out every affected group, the provisions that carry the most risk, and the recommended adjustments in one place.

::: notes
Keep this slide short. The point is the timing of the cost: after, instead of before.
:::

# What is different here

- This is **not** a concept, a mock-up or a slideware promise. It is a built, deployed platform that runs today in an ordinary web browser.
- It models **150 stakeholder groups** (40 per institution) and holds **510 reference indicators** across **16 government institutions**.
- The same department and the same policy text always produce a **byte-identical** result — no randomness, no clock in the result.
- The simulation runs **entirely on the reader's own machine** — no third-party script and no outside font is ever fetched. The parts written by the AI come from the **Nzwisiso AI Policy Simulation Engine®**, which the platform calls over an interface and which runs on **Oreida Pvt Ltd's own servers** under the licence.

::: notes
The differentiating claim is reproducibility plus sovereignty. Say both.
:::

# What already exists

- **16 government institutions**, from the Office of the President and Cabinet to ZIDA.
- **150 modelled stakeholder groups** — 20 stand on a published national share, 130 are labelled `Modelled`.
- **510 reference indicators** — 275 are published figures naming their publisher and period, 235 are `Modelled`.
- **48 prepared policy drafts** (three per institution), ready to run today.
- **A complete officer journey:** choose a department → upload or paste a draft → run → read the assessment → export → generate a drafted policy.

::: notes
These are coverage figures, not proof of savings. Do not present them as outcomes.
:::

# A live demonstration, not a promise

- We can demonstrate the whole journey today, on a machine the Ministry already owns, with no installation.
- **Click order for a live run:** (1) choose a department; (2) pick a prepared draft; (3) press **Run Simulation**; (4) watch the modelled population and its relationships build up; (5) open the executive summary; (6) open the full assessment; (7) export to PDF or Word; (8) generate the drafted policy.
- **If the network is unavailable:** the simulation still runs in full, because it is computed on the reader's own machine. Only the AI-written parts (the drafted policy, the research answers) need the engine to be reachable — so **do the live demonstration with connectivity**. *(Corrected 2026-10-11: this bullet previously said the platform "makes no network request", which stopped being true once the AI keys were built in.)*

# What one run produces

- An **executive summary** for a reader in a hurry.
- A **long-form report** and a **full assessment**, with the modelled groups, the stated priorities, the risks raised, and the recommended steps.
- A **drafted policy** — a real instrument with numbered provisions — that the officer can then edit.
- Every figure marked for review: published figures name their source; everything else is plainly labelled `Modelled`.

::: notes
Stress that the output is written for a human to read, challenge and act on.

# The governance boundary

- The platform states, word for word, that it **does not replace policymakers or determine policy outcomes**.
- It provides an **additional analytical lens** to support informed human judgement.
- Every generated document carries a **decision-support disclaimer**.
- This is scenario analysis that is reproducible and reviewable — **not** a prediction of public opinion, and never "the AI decided".

::: notes
This is the honesty slide. Do not soften it.

# Why this is the pilot for Digitalize Zimbabwe

- **Digitalize Zimbabwe** is a named initiative of this Ministry, launched by the Honourable Minister at Domboshava in April 2024, with a **2030** horizon — internet access and personal computers for citizens, young people first.
- It names five workstreams, including **e-Government services**; Cabinet approved the **Presidential Internet Scheme** for **all 2,400 administrative wards** (April 2025); and the Government states it is **committed to full digitalisation by 2030**.
- In June 2026 the Government launched the **National Artificial Intelligence Strategy** — priority sectors **healthcare, agriculture, education and financial inclusion**.
- What does not yet exist is a **working, sovereign example that the Ministry itself can put to work**. This platform is exactly that, one step short.
- **No endorsement is held.** The platform holds **no** endorsement from the Ministry or the programme; obtaining one is part of the ask.

# How this compares

- Zimbabwe's Ministry of Finance had a financial-management system project appraised at **US$20 million** and signed at **US$10 million** — and the Government pays **approximately US$3 million a year** just to maintain that system *(World Bank, Zimbabwe: Public Financial Management Enhancement Project, ICR00005623, 4 February 2022)*.
- Comparable national platforms built by other African governments cost **US$1.45 million to US$7.8 million** to design and build, with annual running of **US$155,000 to US$873,000** *(World Bank, Electronic Government Procurement — Implementation Types: Options for Africa, 2022, pages 99–110)*.
- **Zimbabwe's own budget** allocated **US$1.7 million** to establishing **one** information system — the automated mining cadastre — in 2018, and it was **never spent** *(Government of Zimbabwe National Budget Statement 2019)*.
- Our price sits **inside both published ranges**: a one-off of **US$2,800,000**, and **US$72,000 a month** that includes hosting, hardware, power, connectivity, support and updates — where the comparators' annual figures **excluded** hosting.
- The published record on building it yourself: large IT projects run **45% over budget on average**, and **18% of IT projects overrun by more than 50%** — those by **447%**.

::: notes
Lead with Zimbabwe's own US$20 million appraisal and the US$3 million a year maintenance line: it is our own Government's own figure, published by the World Bank, and nobody can argue with it. Then the African band. Then the decisive point — our monthly includes hosting, hardware, power and connectivity that theirs excluded.
