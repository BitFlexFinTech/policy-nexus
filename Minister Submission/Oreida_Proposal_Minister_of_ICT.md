## Part B — Memorandum for Decision

### 1. Recommendation

1.1 That the Ministry approve a **pilot** of the Nzwisiso AI™ policy-simulation platform, endorsed as a pilot for the **Digitalize Zimbabwe** initiative, hosted inside the Ministry, and funded for the three named build steps below.

1.2 That the pilot be assessed on the results of **one institution, one policy question and one run**, before any national rollout is committed to.

1.3 That the Ministry, if it approves in principle, direct the pilot to begin with the three build steps named at paragraph 9.2 — namely server-side document text extraction, server-side identity verification, and deployment onto Government infrastructure.

### 2. Background

2.1 The platform is a browser-based tool for testing a draft policy before it is implemented. It is the proof of concept for a proposed national capability, referred to in this proposal as the **Zimbabwe AI Policy Intelligence Initiative** and delivered on the **Nzwisiso AI™** platform.

2.2 The platform informs a decision; it does not take one. It produces a structured, reproducible scenario analysis for a human reader, and it is designed so that the same department and the same policy text always produce a byte-identical result.

2.3 The platform is reachable today at a demonstration address. It does **not** run on Government infrastructure, and nothing in this proposal should be read as saying that it does.

### 3. The problem it addresses

3.1 Policies are frequently implemented without a structured way of examining the likely responses of the groups they affect. The cost of a provision that is not understood — resistance, delay, rework, or a measure that cannot be delivered as written — is discovered **after** implementation rather than before it.

3.2 Departments hold real policy expertise, but they have no standard tool that lays out, in one place, how each affected group is likely to respond, which provisions carry the greatest risk, and what the recommended adjustments would be.

3.3 The platform answers that gap in two ways: it states plainly what a draft policy will do to whom, and it produces a written assessment a person can read, challenge and act upon.

### 4. What has already been delivered

4.1 A **working platform**, built and deployed, used in a web browser. No installation on a department's machines is required, and no server is needed to demonstrate it.

4.2 **Coverage of 16 government institutions:** the Office of the President and Cabinet; the Ministry of Finance, Economic Development and Investment Promotion; the Ministry of Lands, Agriculture and Water; the Ministry of Health and Child Care; the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education; the Ministry of Higher and Tertiary Education, Innovation, Science and Technology Development; the Ministry of Information Communication Technology, Postal and Courier Services; the Ministry of Mines and Mining Development; the Ministry of Energy and Power Development; the Public Service Commission; the Ministry of Local Government and Public Works; the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Trade; the Ministry of Environment, Climate and Wildlife; the Ministry of Defence and War Veterans Affairs; the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority; and the Zimbabwe Investment and Development Agency.

4.3 **72 modelled stakeholder groups**, including civil servants, urban and rural households, informal traders, manufacturers, mining operators, smallholder farmers, the diaspora, youth, women, pensioners, the financial sector, exporters, local authorities, development partners, health workers, educators, traditional leaders, faith-based organisations, trade unions, employer federations, cooperatives, the media, tourism and transport operators, energy and water utilities, and persons with disabilities. **20 of the 72 stand on a published national share**, each naming the figure it stands for and the publication it came from; the remaining **52 are explicitly labelled `Modelled`**, because no published count exists. Each department models the **24 groups** its own mandate covers, drawn from these 72.

4.4 **160 reference indicators** across those 16 institutions, each with a plain note and a stated basis: **24 are published figures**, each naming its publisher, its publication and its period, and **136 are explicitly labelled `Modelled`**, because no publisher publishes that measure. These figures describe what the platform holds; they are not evidence of savings or outcomes.

4.5 **48 prepared policy drafts** — three per institution — ready to be run today, so a demonstration needs no preparation by the department being shown.

4.6 **49 reference documents** and **64 stated institutional priorities** held in the same configuration. Each document entry cites a **real, verified instrument** — a named Act, Statutory Instrument, policy or strategy, with a chapter number only where the official consolidated index confirms one. The platform holds the register and the citations, **not the document files**.

4.7 **A complete officer journey that works end to end:** choose a department; upload or paste a policy draft; run the simulation; watch the modelled population and its relationships build up live; read an executive summary, a long-form report and a full assessment; export to PDF, Word or print; and generate an editable drafted policy from the run.

4.8 **Reproducibility is a design property, not a claim:** the same department and the same policy text always produce a byte-identical result. There is no randomness and no clock in the result, which is what makes a finding defensible in a meeting.

4.9 **The assessment runs entirely in the reader's own browser.** The demonstration build makes **no network request at all** — no external AI service is called, no document text leaves the machine, and no third-party script is loaded. That is what makes it sovereign in operation: nothing is sent anywhere to be processed.

4.10 **The governance boundary is built into the product.** The platform states, word for word, that it does not replace policymakers or determine policy outcomes, and that it provides an additional analytical lens to support informed human judgement. Every generated document carries a decision-support disclaimer.

### 5. The pilot case — Digitalize Zimbabwe

5.1 **Digitalize Zimbabwe** is a named initiative of this Ministry. It was launched by the Honourable Minister at Domboshava in April 2024, with a stated horizon of **2030** — internet access and personal computers for citizens, young people first — and it unveiled the **Digitalize Zimbabwe Magazine** and the **Presidential Internet Scheme**.

5.2 The initiative sits inside the **Zimbabwe National ICT Policy 2022–2027** framework and names five workstreams: expanding digital infrastructure; affordable devices with rural areas first; **e-Government** services; digital literacy training; and innovation and entrepreneurship.

5.3 In April 2025, Cabinet approved implementation of the **Presidential Internet Scheme**, bringing reliable broadband to **all 2,400 administrative wards**. The Government has stated it is **committed to full digitalisation by 2030**, now framed under **NDS2 (2026–2030)** and **Vision 2030**.

5.4 In June 2026 the Government launched the **National Artificial Intelligence Strategy**, whose named priority sectors are **healthcare, agriculture, education and financial inclusion**, and which commits to AI that reflects Zimbabwe's linguistic and cultural diversity.

5.5 The Government has therefore already decided that Zimbabwe will digitalise public administration, and the AI Strategy sets the direction. What does not yet exist is a **working, sovereign example that the Ministry itself hosts**. This platform is exactly that, one step short.

5.6 The platform answers the programme's own criteria in one product: applied AI in a public service (policy assessment); reproducible and reviewable results; and **every policy text and every result kept inside national custody** — the sovereignty property the programme is built on.

5.7 What is proposed is a **pilot**: one institution, assessed on its results, before any national rollout is committed to. This is a proposal put to the programme's owners, **not** an announcement of their decision. The Ministry holds **no** endorsement of this platform, and obtaining one is part of the ask.

### 6. Governance and legal position

6.1 The platform informs; a human decides. Every result is reproducible and reviewable; no document text leaves the machine on which it runs; and every generated document carries the decision-support disclaimer. A legal instrument would be drafted **only after** approval; no legal instrument is annexed to this proposal.

### 9. Implementation approach and timeframe

9.1 The approach is phased, and the **pilot institution is named as the first milestone**.

### 10. Risk and mitigation

10.1 **Reputational risk of overclaiming.** A tool of this kind fails when it is described as a prediction. The product design mitigates this: figures that are not published are labelled `Modelled`; every result is reproducible; and every document carries the decision-support disclaimer.

10.2 **The specific risk of borrowing a national programme's name.** The platform holds **no** endorsement from **Digitalize Zimbabwe** or the Ministry. This proposal states that plainly, and obtaining the endorsement is part of the ask.

10.3 **Data and sovereignty risk.** The demonstration makes no network request and no document text leaves the machine, which removes the risk of policy text being processed outside national custody.
