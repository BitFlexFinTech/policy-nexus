## What this page is for, in one paragraph

The Ministry will ask one question that decides everything: **is this price reasonable?** Most suppliers
answer that by explaining their own costs. That is the weakest possible answer, because the Ministry is
not buying costs — it is buying a system, and it can compare systems. This page answers the question the
way the Ministry will actually ask it: **what have other African governments paid for platforms of this
kind, published and in writing, and what has Zimbabwe's own Government already been willing to spend on
a single information system?** It is laid out in the order agreed for the deck and the proposal —
**the price, then the proof, then who pays, then the ask.**

## 2. The proof — what other African governments actually paid, published and page-cited

**The source, named and checkable:** *Electronic Government Procurement — Implementation Types: Options
for Africa*, **World Bank, 2022** (155 pages). It reports real, interviewed project costs, not estimates.

| Country and system | Total | Design and build | Annual running | Running ÷ build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mauritius** (p110) | $2.98M | **$1.45M** | **$300,000 a year** | 20.7% |
| **Rwanda** — UMUCYO (p99) | $12M invested | **$7.8M** | **$800,000 a year** *(includes hosting, hardware, new features and user support)* | 10.3% |
| **Tunisia** — TUNEPS (p104) | $5.7M (2011) | **$4.8M** | **$155,000 a year** *(includes $72,000 of hosting)* | 3.2% |
| **Uganda** (p106 / p109) | $4.15M | **$1.55M** | **$873,000 a year** to European Dynamics, **excluding hosting — and that contract was terminated in 2021, the government dissatisfied** | ~56% |

**Also real, for scale:**
- **The Gambia — IFMIS:** total **US$5.0M**, of which rollout, interfaces and training **US$3.34M**,

**The band these figures create, and it is the band that justifies a price:** one-off build
**$1.45M–$7.8M** · annual running **$155,000–$873,000** · total programme **$2.98M–$12M**.

## 3. Zimbabwe's own anchor — one system, one budget line, and it was never spent

> *"In the 2018 national budget government allocated **US$1.7 million** towards the establishment of the
> automated mining cadastre information system which was not utilised due to foreign currency
> constraints (GoZ National Budget Statement 2019)."*

**Read this session, word for word, out of the project's own held research corpus**
(`public/zepari-corpus.json`, ZEPARI's own research citing the **Government of Zimbabwe National Budget
Statement 2019**). It is the strongest single line on the page, because it is **Zimbabwe's own money,
budgeted for one information system, eight years ago** — and it was more than the whole of Option C put
together. It removes the "this is a foreign price" objection before it is made.

## 4. What is genuinely different about this offer — and it is true

In **every** published comparator above, **the government pays for the hosting and the hardware itself**:
Mauritius runs its own cloud; Rwanda and Uganda are both hosted at NITA-U, their own national IT agency;
Tunisia is hosted at its National Computer Centre.

**Ours is not like that.** This price **includes** 8 Mac Studio servers, a spare server, a Mac mini,
AppleCare cover, solar panels, a 10 kVA inverter, a 22 kWh battery, cooling, a 1 Gbps line, a backup
line, RAID backup and the training of the department's own officers. Those are costs every comparator
government carried **on top of** the figures in section 2.

That is the whole argument, and it needs no exaggeration: **the comparison is favourable because the
offer is broader, not because the other figures are wrong.**

## 5. The recommendation

1. **Raise the one-off, or hold it at Option A.** $1.8M–$2.5M sits inside the published build band
   ($1.45M–$7.8M) and is arguably still cheap for what is included.
2. **Do not raise the monthly above the published band.** $65,000–$70,000 a month is the highest the
   evidence supports; Option A's $72,000 is a rounding away from it, and it is defensible on the ground
   that hosting, hardware, power and connectivity are inside it where the comparators' were not.
3. **Argue on term, not on rate.** The monthly is what re-opens a contract (Uganda is the proof). A
   longer stated term raises the total without ever stretching the annual figure.
4. **Never justify the price by cost-plus**, and never show the Ministry the internal cost sheet.
