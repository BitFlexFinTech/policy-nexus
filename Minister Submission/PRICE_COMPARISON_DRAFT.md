## The one-paragraph answer to "is this reasonable?"

**Yes, and the strongest evidence is Zimbabwe's own.** The Ministry of Finance is, on the World Bank's own
published record, paying **approximately US$3 million a year** simply to maintain one financial system —
and it was appraised at **US$20 million** to improve it. Against that, **$2,800,000 to buy a working,
sovereign policy-assessment platform outright, and $864,000 a year all-in to have Oreida Pvt Ltd run,
host, power, maintain and support it, is small.** The one-off is **14%** of the Ministry's own appraised
figure for one system, and the yearly cost is **29%** of what the Ministry already pays in maintenance
alone — for a platform covering **16 institutions**. Every figure below is named and published.
**Nothing here is invented, and nothing here is an estimate of ours.**

## 3. Why a fixed price with a monthly licence is the RIGHT route — the failure evidence

The Ministry will be tempted to say "we could build this ourselves". **The published evidence says that is
the most expensive route.** Every figure below is published and named.

| Evidence | The figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| IT projects that overrun badly, and by how much | **18% of IT projects exceed budget by more than 50%; those overrun by an average of 447%** | *The Empirical Reality of IT Project Cost Overruns: Discovering A Power-Law Distribution*, **Journal of Management Information Systems, Vol 39, Issue 3** (Flyvbjerg and co-authors); reported by **strategy+business (PwC), 19 June 2023** |
| Large IT projects | **run 45% over budget on average**; **17% go so badly wrong they threaten the organisation's existence** | **McKinsey & Company**, *Delivering large-scale IT projects on time, on budget, and on value* (2012) |
| Projects that fail or are challenged | **69%** — 19% failed outright, 50% challenged (over budget, late, or under-delivered) | **The Standish Group, CHAOS Report 2020** |
| Where IT budgets actually go | **55%–80% of IT budgets are spent maintaining systems that already exist** | widely attributed to **Gartner** |
| What Africa's own record shows | governments have "**struggled to procure, build, and institutionalize e-GP systems despite millions of dollars in headline Public Financial Management reforms from donors**" | **Open Contracting Partnership, 27 February 2024** |
| And Zimbabwe's own recent attempt | scope **cut from US$20 million to US$10 million**; licences **90% idle**; the system **shut down over arrears** | **World Bank ICR00005623** (section 1) |

**The argument this supports, and it is a fair one:** a **fixed price for software that already works**,
plus a **monthly fee the Ministry can see and stop**, transfers the overrun risk to Oreida Pvt Ltd. That
is what the Government is buying with the monthly — not just hosting and support, but **the risk of
another US$20-million project that never lands.**

## 4. What governments pay for comparable capability elsewhere

| Buyer | What it bought | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US Government Accountability Office** | enterprise IT services (SAIC) | **$95 million** | GovConWire, February 2026 |
| **US Department of Homeland Security** | RAPID IT support services (recompete) | **$100 million** | GovConWire, April 2025 |
| **US Department of Defense**, Chief Digital and AI Office | AI capability, four vendors | **up to $200 million each** (Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, xAI) | MarketScale, reporting a Forrester analysis, 2026 |
| **Annual maintenance benchmark** | the cost of keeping software alive, per year | **10–25% of build cost in years 1–2; 15–30% in years 3–5; 20–40% from year 6 onward** | widely attributed to **Gartner** |

**Read that last row against our figures:** on a $2,800,000 platform, maintenance alone is
**$280,000–$700,000 a year** by that benchmark — **before** hosting, hardware, power, connectivity, support
and updates. Ours is **$864,000 a year with all of it included.** The two are not comparable, and that is
the honest reason our figure is where it is.
